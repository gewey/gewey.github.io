Company — every name leveling beside you

The social residue of a server. DingFanfare renders a congratulation card on every level-up it witnesses and keeps the card forever; AdventurersLog notes who was in the party when a dungeon ended. Read together they are a census, not a diary.

Every other wing in this station counts one hunter. This one counts the room he leveled in: 321 names that DingFanfare kept a card for — 247 guild, 66 pickup group and 8 friend — the 130 level-up dings it overheard, the 11 realm-first levels it timed, the 17 dungeon companions AdventurersLog wrote down, and 5,041 player names pulled straight out of the four Forever combat logs.

02The pulsewhen a server levels: dings by day, dings by the clock

DingFanfare stamps every card with the epoch it fired. Grouped by day and by hour, the tape becomes a pulse: 130 dings across 2 tracked days, peaking on 2026-11-18 with 74. The busiest clock hour is 21:00 — 50 dings. A /played counter cannot show this; a card index can. One caveat keeps it honest: the 21:00 bar is the roster scan firing, not a stampede of level-ups, so treat the flat daytime band as the real live witnessing rate.

The day strip needs three or more dated days to draw a shape — there are 2 (the addon is younger than the character), so the hour strip below carries the pulse instead.

Method. Generated, not hand-typed. company_extract.py tokenises two SavedVariables with the same Lua reader almanac_extract.py uses and derives every figure on this page from them. Four rules kept it honest: the people table is read whole, so no name is sampled out; a ding is counted only when its key parses as name|L|level, so a malformed card is dropped rather than guessed at; a class colour is painted only when the same name still exists in the people table (that is why 130 of 130 dings carry one); and firsts are printed with the addon's own verb, learned, never relabelled as "server first" because the addon only knows what it saw.

Three sources feed it: DingFanfare.lua (321 cards, 130 dings, 100 history rows), AdventurersLog.lua (17 companions) and the four Logs\WoWCombatLog-*.txt files (164,213 lines, 5,041 player GUIDs, 19 of them shared with the card index). A log name is joined to a card on the name only -- first and last, hyphenated in the log and spaced on the roll -- because the log carries no class and the card carries no GUID; that is why the combat-roll table prints the class only where the join succeeded, and why it is called a match rather than an identity.
